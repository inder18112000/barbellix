## 1. Executive Summary

The AI-Powered Fitness Ecosystem ("the Platform") is a multi-tenant, B2B2C fitness and coaching
system that pairs generative-AI programming with certified-trainer oversight. It serves four
distinct audiences from one data platform: **members** who train, **trainers** who coach, **gym
admins** who run the business, and a **super admin** who runs the platform itself. Its
differentiating claim against every existing fitness app on the market is not "AI generates your
workout" — every competitor already does that — but that **the AI keeps regenerating your workout**,
on a schedule, from real progress data, with a human trainer in the loop who can approve, edit, or
override every change before the member ever sees it.

Three properties define the Platform and recur throughout this document set:

1. **Closed-loop programming, not one-shot generation.** A workout or meal plan is never a static
   artifact. It is versioned. Every N days (7 by default, configurable per user or per gym), the
   system compares the member's new progress snapshot against their last one and proposes a
   specific, explainable set of changes — more sets, fewer carbs, an added recovery day — not a
   fresh plan from scratch.
2. **AI proposes, a certified human disposes.** Every AI output that reaches a member has a
   provenance: AI-generated, trainer-edited, or trainer-authored. Nothing generated by a model
   reaches a paying member without having passed through an assigned trainer's review queue, unless
   that member has no assigned trainer (Free tier), in which case the system discloses this plainly
   in-product.
3. **One platform, four cockpits.** The member experience (mobile-first), the trainer cockpit
   (mobile + web), the gym admin console (web), and the super admin control plane (web) are four
   different products built on one shared data model, one auth system, and one AI layer — not four
   codebases that happen to talk to the same database.

This document set specifies that platform end-to-end: personas and business model (this part),
roles and the canonical data model (Part 1), every client application (Parts 2–3), the trainer
and AI-review workflow (Part 4), the recommendation engine itself (Part 5), content libraries and
progress/wearables (Part 6), monetization/notifications/gamification/analytics (Part 7), the
database and service architecture (Parts 8–9), security and compliance (Part 9), and the phased
build-out and future roadmap (Part 10).

## 2. Vision & Objectives

**Vision statement.** *Every person training toward a physical goal should have a coaching program
that adapts to their real, measured progress at least as often as they check in on it — and every
adaptation should be safe, explainable, and reviewable by a qualified human.*

**Strategic objectives, in priority order:**

| # | Objective | What "done" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prove the closed-loop adaptive engine end-to-end | A member completes two consecutive check-in cycles and receives a *materially different, explainable* plan version each time, with a visible diff against the prior version |
| 2 | Keep trainers in the loop without them becoming a bottleneck | Median AI-plan review turnaround under 24 hours; a trainer can review a proposed change in under 60 seconds for the common case (accept as-is) |
| 3 | Make the platform sellable to gyms, not just individuals | A gym admin can onboard a branch, invite trainers, and see per-trainer / per-branch adherence and revenue reporting without engineering involvement |
| 4 | Keep member data safe and access provably scoped | Every cross-role data access is enforced at the API layer and independently auditable — see Part 9 |
| 5 | Leave room for the roadmap without a rewrite | Video/CV features, wearables, and voice coaching (Part 10) attach to existing services rather than requiring new core data models |

**Non-goals (explicitly out of scope for the core product, revisited in Part 10):**

- Diagnosing or treating medical conditions. The AI Chat Coach gives fitness/nutrition guidance
  with mandatory injury/medical disclaimers and an escalation path to the assigned trainer or,
  where none exists, a "see a professional" deflection — it is not a substitute for medical advice.
- Real-time computer-vision form correction and rep-counting from live video. Scoped as a Phase 2+
  capability (Part 10), not core.
- Building a payments processor. The Platform integrates a PCI-compliant provider (Stripe) rather
  than handling card data directly (Part 7, Part 9).

## 3. Business Model

The Platform is **B2B2C**: gyms and independent trainers are the primary sales motion, members are
the primary usage motion, and revenue is collected at both layers.

### 3.1 Revenue streams

| Stream | Payer | What they get | Billing shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Member subscriptions** | Individual member | Free / Premium tiers (§3.2) | Recurring, monthly or annual, self-serve |
| **Trainer seats** | Independent trainer or gym | Client-management tools, AI-assisted plan authoring, analytics | Per-seat recurring |
| **Gym Enterprise** | Gym/franchise owner | Multi-branch, multi-trainer, white-label branding, consolidated reporting | Per-branch or per-active-member tiered, annual contract common |
| **Usage-based AI overage** | Gym Enterprise accounts | AI generation calls beyond a plan's included quota | Metered add-on |
| **Marketplace / sponsorship** | Brands, supplement companies | Sponsored placements in the content feed and Sponsor directory | Flat-fee or CPM placement, admin/super-admin managed |

### 3.2 Subscription tiers (member-facing)

| Tier | Price posture | Included |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | 1 AI plan generation per interval cycle, basic progress tracking, AI Chat Coach with a daily message cap, no assigned trainer (self-serve plans are not trainer-reviewed and the product discloses this) |
| **Premium** | Paid, monthly/annual | Unlimited AI regenerations, full progress analytics, advanced reports, full nutrition planning, unlimited AI Chat Coach, wearable sync |
| **Trainer Plan** | Paid, per trainer seat | Everything a Trainer role needs: client roster, plan builder, exercise/meal authoring, adherence analytics, messaging |
| **Gym Enterprise** | Contract, per branch/member-band | Multiple branches, multiple trainer seats, white-label theming, consolidated cross-branch reporting, priority support, dedicated AI usage quota |

Feature gating is enforced server-side against the member's/tenant's active tier — see Part 9 §2
for the enforcement pattern (a common gap in earlier builds is *storing* the tier without ever
*checking* it; this spec treats tier-gating as a first-class authorization concern, not a display
concern).

### 3.3 Multi-tenancy model

The Platform is tenant-scoped at the **gym/organization** level (a "Tenant" in Part 1's data
model). An independent trainer with no gym affiliation is modeled as a tenant of one. A Gym
Enterprise tenant has multiple **Branches**, each with its own roster, trainers, and
branch-level reporting rolled up to the tenant. All data — members, plans, exercises, messages —
carries a `tenantId` and, where relevant, a `branchId`, and no query path may cross tenant
boundaries except for the Super Admin role acting through explicitly audited platform-operations
endpoints (Part 9).

## 4. User Personas

### Persona 1 — "Ritika," the Member

- **Age 29, marketing manager, Bengaluru.** Trains 4x/week, goal: body recomposition. Owns a
  fitness tracker (Apple Watch). Has tried three fitness apps before this one and abandoned all
  three within six weeks because the plan never changed even as her lifts and body changed.
- **What she needs from the Platform:** a plan that visibly reacts to her actual progress, a
  trainer she can message when something feels wrong (a nagging knee, a plateau), and enough
  structure (reminders, streaks) to keep showing up on weeks she's not motivated.
- **What would make her churn:** a plan that feels identical after a month of consistent logging;
  an AI chat answer that ignores an injury she already told the app about.

### Persona 2 — "Arjun," the Gym Trainer

- **Age 34, certified strength coach, employed by a 3-branch gym chain.** Manages 40 assigned
  clients. Spends his current (pre-Platform) evenings manually rewriting spreadsheets.
- **What he needs from the Platform:** every AI-generated plan for *his* clients (and only his
  clients) in one review queue, fast accept/edit actions, a way to see who's falling behind before
  they quit, and full override authority — the AI should accelerate his judgment, not replace it.
- **What would make him distrust the Platform:** an AI recommendation he can't see the reasoning
  for; being shown clients who aren't actually assigned to him; a plan going to his client without
  his review.

### Persona 3 — "Meena," the Gym Admin (Owner/Manager)

- **Age 41, owns a 2-branch gym.** Non-technical, cares about occupancy, renewal rates, and
  trainer performance, not about AI internals.
- **What she needs from the Platform:** a dashboard she checks every morning (active members,
  expiring subscriptions, pending payments), the ability to approve new trainers and moderate
  anything a member reports, and confidence that billing "just works."
- **What would make her cancel the contract:** payment reconciliation she can't trust; a trainer
  approval process that lets anyone claim the trainer role.

### Persona 4 — "Devraj," the Super Admin

- **Platform operator, works for the company running the Platform, not for any one gym.**
  Responsible for the health of the AI layer itself, the prompt library, feature rollout, and the
  platform's compliance posture across every tenant.
- **What he needs from the Platform:** a global RBAC and feature-flag console, an AI prompt library
  with versioning (so a prompt regression is one rollback away, not a redeploy), an immutable audit
  log across every tenant, and backup/restore he can actually test.
- **What would make him escalate to engineering at 2 a.m.:** a tenant boundary that leaked; an AI
  provider outage with no fallback; an audit log with a gap in it.

### Persona 5 — "Aditi," the Franchise/Enterprise Buyer

- **Runs an 8-branch regional gym franchise, evaluating the Platform as a Trainerize/Mindbody
  alternative.** Cares about branding parity across branches, per-branch P&L visibility, and
  whether onboarding 8 branches' worth of trainers and members is a project or a button.
- **What she needs from the Platform:** the Gym Enterprise tier (§3.2) to actually deliver
  multi-branch reporting and white-label theming on day one of the contract, not as a future
  promise.
